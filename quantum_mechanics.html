Perspective of Quantum Mechanics from Chemistry to Math

From my perspective, I have had a pretty unusual progression on how I have learned microscopic properties of the universe. I started with a bachelors in Chemistry, currently working on a P.h.D in Chemical Physics and most recently, have become a MathQuantum fellow. Through this progression of learning, I believe I have also contemplated on what I am learning and what I have a learned, and what I have taken for granted in my earlier course work. In this page I am hoping to outline how it all started, and where I am at today, starting from (what I understand) to be a physcial chemists knowledge on quantum mechanics, to a physcist, and then finally from a pure mathematical ground of QM.

Principles of Quantum chemistry

Most problems in chemistry look to use the tools of quantum mechanics to describe the microscopic properties of molecules. This has obviously has a range of problems that can be solved using these problems. To name a few, molecular stability in aromatic compounds, spectroscopic properties, collisional dynamics and chemical reactivity. To being describe these processes, the first step is usually the Born-Oppenheimer approximation.
