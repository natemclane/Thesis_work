Nuclear spin coupling in CH₄ — conversion between spin species

Spin species are A (I = 2), F (I = 1) and E (I = 0). Only interactions that couple the nuclear spins to the rotation can convert one species into another, and they must change the rotational state at the same time so the total symmetry is conserved.

1. Spin-rotation Hamiltonian

HSR = Σi Ii · Ci · J
= Σi [ C⊥ Ii·J + (C∥ − C⊥) (Ii·ni)(J·ni) ]

Ii is the spin of proton i, J the rotational angular momentum, ni the unit vector along the C–H bond i (body frame), and C∥, C⊥ the two independent components of the spin-rotation tensor for a C₃ᵥ site.

2. Spin-spin (dipolar) Hamiltonian

HSS = Σi<j (μ₀/4π)(γ²ħ²/rij³) [ Ii·Ij − 3 (Ii·r̂ij)(Ij·r̂ij) ]

r̂ij is the unit vector along the H–H direction. Its lab-frame components come from the body-frame ones through direction cosines (D1 functions), so the product of two of them is a D2 function.

3. Vector model

In the vector model two angular momenta a and b precess about their sum F = a + b. The coupling energy is proportional to a·b = ½ [F(F+1) − a(a+1) − b(b+1)], which fixes the angle between the vectors. Pick a value of F below.

The vector model shows the diagonal (coupled) picture. Conversion comes from the off-diagonal matrix elements of HSR and HSS, which mix states with different I and different J. Both terms leave the total symmetry unchanged.

The indirect scalar coupling Jij Ii·Ij is not a conversion mechanism: for four equivalent protons it depends only on the total I, so it never mixes spin species.
