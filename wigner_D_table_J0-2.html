Wigner D-matrices and rigid-rotor wavefunctions, J = 0, 1, 2

Convention: DJMK(α,β,γ) = e−iMα dJMK(β) e−iKγ, and ψJKM(α,β,γ) = NJ · DJ*MK with NJ = √((2J+1)/8π²). M = projection of J on the lab z-axis, K = projection on the body-fixed axis. d-matrix phases follow the standard (Sakurai/Edmonds) convention. Normalization: ∫₀2πdα ∫₀πsinβ dβ ∫₀2πdγ |ψ|² = 1. For a spherical top such as CH₄, E = BJ(J+1), independent of K and M.

The 2⁴ = 16 nuclear spin states of four spin-½ H nuclei split into A (I = 2, 5 states), F (I = 1, 9 states) and E (I = 0, 2 states). The H nuclei are identical fermions, so the total function must be symmetric under the molecule's proper rotations (they permute the H nuclei evenly). Rotational and nuclear-spin symmetry species must therefore match: A↔A, F↔F, E↔E. Consequences: the all-up state (I = 2, MI = +2) can only occur with A rotational levels (J = 0, 3, …), not with J = 1 or J = 2. For J = 2 the five K-combinations per M split into E (K = 0 and one K = ±2 combination; the two E functions) and F₂ (the other K = ±2 combination and the K = ±1 combinations; the three F₂ functions), and these couple to different spin species. The individual (M, K) rows below are not symmetry-adapted, so the nuclear spin partner is listed per J level.
